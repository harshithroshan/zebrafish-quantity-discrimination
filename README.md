# Quantity Discrimination in Zebrafish

Research assistantship, 2025. Neuroethology Lab, Ashoka University.

**Research question.** Can larval zebrafish discriminate between quantities, and
do their choices follow a ratio rule rather than continuous cues such as surface
area?

Quantity discrimination is well documented across animal taxa: fish
discriminate larger from smaller shoals, and their accuracy is ratio-dependent,
particularly at higher quantities, consistent with an approximate number
system. The complication is that fish also use non-numerical cues — surface
area, density and other continuous variables — and the behavioral literature
disagrees about how much of an animal's apparent numerosity performance is
numerical rather than perceptual.

A recent line of work in cichlids and stingrays has shown that fish can learn
to associate abstract visual symbols with +1 and −1 operations and then apply
those rules correctly to novel quantities. That proto-arithmetic capacity is
what makes this work worth pursuing.

## Work

- Developed a **dot-splitting paradigm**, which presents equal totals as
  differently sized arrays, and a **ratio-comparison T-maze task**, in which
  the fish chooses between two differently sized arrays.
- Built the apparatus and the visual stimuli used to run them. Dot arrays vary
  in number, dot size and dot shape, so continuous perceptual cues can be
  varied independently of quantity.
- Ran 20+ behavioral trials and coded behavior in BORIS.
- Reviewed 15+ studies on ratio and multiplicative cognition and presented the
  synthesis to the lab; it fed into a publication proposal.

Analysis focuses on choice proportions across arrays, testing whether
preference follows a ratio rule rather than absolute number or surface area.

## Status

Completed. Findings contributed to a publication proposal.
